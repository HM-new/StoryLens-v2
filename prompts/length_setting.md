# Story Length Setting

## Purpose
A content depth selector applied alongside the age transformation. It determines how much of the story to tell. The age band determines how to tell it.

---

## Settings

### Brief
The headline story. What happened, why it matters, what's at stake — nothing more. Core narrative arc only. Minimal background. Two perspectives maximum. Omit detailed timelines, secondary threads, and procedural mechanics.

### Standard
The full story told efficiently. All major facts, context, and narrative threads included. All major perspectives with key evidence. Concise timeline and key details. Trim redundant context and secondary data, but a reader finishes with solid understanding.

### Deep Dive
The complete picture. Essentially all content preserved. Full background, all perspectives with evidence and counter-arguments, complete timeline, key figures, key terms, and key numbers. A reader finishes ready to discuss, debate, or research further.

Step 0: Identify Core Facts
Before assessing parameters or making any cuts, read the full source document and identify 5–10 facts that are essential to a complete understanding of the story at any depth and for any age. These are facts that, if removed, would leave the reader with a materially incomplete picture.
A fact qualifies as core if it meets any of these:

It represents a major action already taken (not just proposed or debated)
It establishes the scale or scope of the story
It provides necessary context for understanding why the story matters now
Removing it would cause the reader to misunderstand the state of affairs

List these facts explicitly. They must appear in the transformed story regardless of length setting or age band. They can be simplified in language but not omitted.
